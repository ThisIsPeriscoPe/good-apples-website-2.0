Kioni

Greek pottery, drawn with microbiomes.

Kioni Skin was founded by Maria Mandroules, a former emergency nurse practitioner and a single mum of four. When two of her children were dealing with debilitating eczema flare ups, she went looking for something that supported the skin barrier rather than just suppressing the symptoms. That search led her to the skin microbiome and the gut, skin and brain axis.

The name comes from Kioni, a village on the Greek island of Ikaria, one of the world's five Blue Zones, where people regularly live past 100 largely free of chronic disease. The brand's whole argument is balance, so the identity had to carry heritage without turning into a museum piece.

The skincare aisle is mostly clinical: sterile, scientific, impersonal. We went the other way. The identity takes the blue and white of classic Mediterranean pottery and fills it with illustrations of microbiomes, cells and germs, so the ancient craft and the modern science sit in the same drawing.
